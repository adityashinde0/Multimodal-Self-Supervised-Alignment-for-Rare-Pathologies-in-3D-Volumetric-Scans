A 3D scan learns to read itself before anyone tells it what to look for.

PS-007 folds 3D CT/MRI volumes into volumetric cubic tokens, randomly masks 75% of them, and trains an asymmetric 3D Masked Autoencoder (3D-MAE) to reconstruct the hidden spatial anatomy. The learned visual representations are aligned with clinical narrative reports via symmetric InfoNCE contrastive learning—enabling zero-shot rare-pathology retrieval.

02

Self-supervised 3D-MAE patch learning

The 16³ scan is partitioned into 4³ volumetric patches (64 tokens). Exactly 75% are masked out (48 tokens). An asymmetric 3D transformer encoder processes only the 16 visible tokens, while a lightweight decoder reconstructs the missing anatomy.

Visible patch (25% / 16 tokens) Masked from encoder (75% / 48 tokens) Reconstructed patch (MSE Loss)
Mask ratio 75%

At a 75% volumetric masking ratio, the encoder only ever sees a quarter of the 3D volume, compelling it to build internal spatial continuity of anatomical structures. Reconstruction loss is evaluated solely on the masked voxels via Mean Squared Error (MSE).

03

Multimodal contrastive alignment

Visual embeddings from the 3D-MAE and text embeddings from the clinical language transformer are projected into a shared 128-dimensional unit hypersphere using symmetric InfoNCE loss.

Radiology report embeddings (128-D)

Visualized via PCA projection to 2D. Matched scan/report pairs pull together along the diagonal while mismatched pairs are repelled with learnable temperature τ = 0.07.
